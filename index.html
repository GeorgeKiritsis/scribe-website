Abstract

      
Scribe is a desktop editor for LaTeX and Python. Write the paper, run the notebook, and the figures and numbers land in the PDF, already up to date. It takes notes with mathematics during a lecture, reads and highlights the papers you cite, and checks the paper before you submit it. It runs on your own computer, and it costs nothing.

    


    

      
Theorem 1 (Price). Scribe is free for everyone, forever.

      
Proof. There is nothing to buy: no account, no subscription, no plan with more compile time or more collaborators. Everything runs on your own computer, so nobody pays to keep a server running.∎

    


    

      
Introduction

      
A paper is rarely just text. Its figures come out of a script, its numbers out of an experiment, and the two drift apart the moment one of them changes. Scribe keeps them together: the notebook and the paper live in one project, and a compile brings every figure and every number up to date.

      
The sections below show what that looks like, recorded from the app itself.

    


    

      

        
Notebooks feed the paper

        
Figures are notebook cells marked [figure: loss]; numbers leave the notebook with nbval("acc", 0.96, err=0.003). In the paper, \nbfig{loss} and \nbval{acc} bring them in, typeset with their uncertainty. Change the code, and the next compile reruns only what went stale.

      

      
    


    

      

        
Notes with mathematics

        
Notes are Markdown files whose maths renders as you write, fast enough for a lecture. Inside a formula, // becomes a fraction, ;a becomes α and -> an arrow; Tab steps through the blanks and out again. Notes link to each other with [[name]], collect #tags, and gather every definition and theorem for revision.

      

      
    


    

      

        
Reading the papers you cite

        
Attach a reference's PDF and read it in Scribe. Select a sentence and press Highlight: the quote goes into your note on that paper, with a link that opens the PDF at its page.

      

      
    


    

      

        
Revision

        
A line starting Q: with its answer on a line starting A: is a card. Review asks the cards that are due and spaces them out by how well you knew them; they also export to Anki.

      

      
    


    

      

        
Tables without the ampersands

        
Put the cursor in a table and edit it as a grid: set each column's alignment, add rows and columns, or paste a block straight from a spreadsheet. Scribe writes back aligned LaTeX, keeping booktabs rules and \multicolumn.

      

      
    


    

      

        
When it doesn't compile

        
Errors come with a line in plain words: which package a command needs, where the unclosed brace is, which citation has no entry. Turn on Compile as you type and the PDF follows your writing.

      

      
    


    

      
Everything else

      
The rest of Scribe, briefly, in Table 1.

      

        

          Table 1: Further features.
          
FeatureWhat it does

          
            
Version historyEvery save keeps a version; see what changed and restore it, no git needed.

            
Check before submittingMissing citations and labels, unreferenced figures, stale results, what arXiv would reject.

Code in notesFenced code highlighted in its language, with a copy button.

            
SyncTeXDouble-click the PDF to reach its source, or highlight text there to find it.

            
Export for submissionOne plain-LaTeX zip a journal can compile, with every value written in.

            
Git, terminal, VimA git panel, a real terminal, and Vim keys if you want them.

          
        

      

    


    

      
Why it exists

      
I wrote Scribe after finishing my thesis. While writing the papers that followed, I found that the tools available didn't suit the way I work, so I decided to build my own, and to give it away.

      
Georgios Kiritsis
